If you think your myGov account was accessed by someone else, act quickly to regain control and check linked services for changes.

## Step 1 — Change your myGov password immediately

Go to [my.gov.au](https://my.gov.au) on a trusted device and change your password straight away. If you can, also sign out other sessions and review recovery options so the attacker can’t reset it again. Use a long, unique password you don’t reuse anywhere else.

## Step 2 — Enable stronger sign-in options

Turn on the strongest sign-in method available to you and remove any unfamiliar devices or options if the service allows it. Check that your recovery email and phone number are correct and under your control. If you use SMS codes and your phone service seems affected, treat it as a possible **SIM swap** and contact your carrier.

## Step 3 — Check linked services for changes

Review each linked service (e.g. **ATO**, **Medicare**, **Centrelink**) for changes to contact details, bank accounts, correspondence preferences, or new notifications. Look for unusual actions like updated addresses, new payment destinations, or new authorised contacts. Take screenshots or notes of anything suspicious.

## Step 4 — Report to Services Australia

If you suspect unauthorised access or can’t regain control, contact **Services Australia** and explain that you believe myGov has been compromised. Ask what they can do to help secure linked services and what reference numbers you should record.  

132 307

## Step 5 — Contact the ATO if tax records may be accessed

If the ATO is linked and you suspect changes or access to your tax record, contact the **ATO** to place appropriate flags and ask about fraud monitoring steps. Keep notes of advice and reference numbers.  

132 861

## Step 6 — Report to the ACSC

Report the incident to the **Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC)** so it’s recorded and you receive relevant guidance.

- **Report:** [cyber.gov.au/report](https://www.cyber.gov.au/report)
