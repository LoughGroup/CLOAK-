A stolen Australian passport can be used for identity fraud or travel in your name. **Cancel it as soon as possible**, then arrange a **replacement** (or an **emergency travel document** if you are overseas and need to travel urgently).

## Step 1 — Report to local police and get a reference number

Report the theft to **local police** in Australia (or overseas police if you are abroad). Request a **reference or event number** — you may need it for insurers or follow-up with authorities.

## Step 2 — Cancel the passport

Cancel the stolen passport through **DFAT** so the document is **invalidated** and harder to misuse.

- **Online:** [passports.dfat.gov.au](https://www.passports.gov.au) (use the official Australian Passport Office channels listed there)  
- **Phone:** **131 232** (within Australia; check DFAT for numbers from overseas)

Follow their instructions for reporting a lost or stolen passport.

## Step 3 — If overseas, contact the nearest Australian consulate

If you are **outside Australia**, contact your **nearest Australian embassy or consulate** for advice on cancellation, security concerns, and how to apply for a **replacement passport** or **emergency passport** depending on your travel needs.

## Step 4 — Apply for a replacement

Lodge a **replacement passport application** as directed by DFAT. If you need to travel before a full passport can be issued, ask about an **emergency passport** — processing times vary, but **emergency travel documents** are often aimed at urgent situations (commonly around **two business days** where circumstances meet DFAT’s criteria; confirm current timeframes when you apply).
