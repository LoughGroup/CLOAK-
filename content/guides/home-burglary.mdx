After a burglary, your first priorities are **safety** and preserving evidence, then documenting losses for police and insurance.

## Step 1 — Don’t enter if you think someone may still be inside

If you see signs of forced entry or you suspect someone is still in the home, don’t go in. Move to a safe place and call **000**. Your safety comes before property recovery.

## Step 2 — Call police and follow their instructions

If the situation is not immediate danger but you’ve discovered a break-in, contact police via your local reporting channels or **131 444** (non-urgent). Ask what you should and shouldn’t touch before they attend. Request an event/reference number for insurance and follow-on reporting.

## Step 3 — Document missing items before touching anything

Take photos of entry points, damaged locks, and any disturbed areas before you start cleaning or moving items. Write a list of missing items, including serial numbers, receipts, or identifying details where possible. This documentation is often the difference between a smooth and frustrating claim process.

## Step 4 — Contact your insurer to start a claim

Contact your insurer and provide the police reference number, photos, and your initial list of missing/damaged items. Ask what emergency repairs they approve (locksmith, boarding up windows) and what receipts they require. Keep a single note with claim number, dates, and who you spoke to.

## Step 5 — Change locks if keys were taken (or access was compromised)

If keys, garage remotes, access fobs, or spare keys were taken, arrange a **licensed locksmith** to rekey or replace locks promptly. Even if keys weren’t taken, consider lock changes if doors/windows were forced or damaged. Inform your landlord/property manager if you rent.

## Step 6 — Contact IDCARE if personal documents were stolen

If passports, licences, Medicare cards, bank letters, or other identity documents were taken, contact **IDCARE** for tailored identity-risk steps and sequencing.
