When luggage goes missing, time matters: reports filed at the airport and accurate contents lists make recovery and claims much easier.

## Step 1 — If at an airport, lodge a Property Irregularity Report before leaving

Go to the airline baggage desk and lodge a **Property Irregularity Report (PIR)** before you leave the airport. Confirm the report reference number, the contact method, and expected update timing. Ask whether they’ll deliver to your address or require collection if the bag is found.

## Step 2 — Contact airline baggage services with your booking reference

Call the airline’s baggage services and provide your booking reference, baggage tag number, and PIR details. Ask them to confirm the bag’s last scan location and what their next steps are. Keep a written log of dates, times, names, and reference numbers.

## Step 3 — If the bag contained a passport or key documents, start those guides immediately

If your passport, driver licence, Medicare card, or banking documents were inside, don’t wait for the bag to be found before acting. Start the relevant guides (e.g. passport stolen/lost, identity document theft) and notify issuers to reduce misuse risk. If you’re overseas, consider contacting the nearest Australian consulate early.

## Step 4 — Report to police if stolen (not just misplaced)

If you believe the bag was stolen (e.g. taken from a carousel or vehicle), report it to **police** and obtain an event/reference number. This is often required by insurers and can support investigations. For non-urgent reporting, many states use online reporting or **131 444**.

## Step 5 — Contact your insurer and list contents accurately

Contact your travel insurer (or home and contents insurer, depending on cover) and start a claim with your airline and police reference numbers. Create an itemised list of contents with approximate values and any receipts/photos you have. Accurate documentation speeds assessment and reduces disputes.

## Step 6 — Track using AirTag or Tile if a tracker was inside

If you had an AirTag or Tile in the bag, check the last known location and movement history in the relevant app. Share location information with airline staff or police rather than confronting anyone directly. Treat tracker data as a lead, not proof, and prioritise safety.
