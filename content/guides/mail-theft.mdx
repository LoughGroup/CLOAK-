If mail or parcels are stolen, act quickly if they contained identity or financial documents that could enable fraud.

## Step 1 — Report to Australia Post

Report the missing mail or parcel to **Australia Post** and ask what tracing, investigation, or proof-of-delivery steps apply. If it was a tracked parcel, provide the tracking number and delivery address details.  

137 678

## Step 2 — Report to police

Lodge a report with **police** (online where available, or call **131 444** for non-urgent assistance), especially if theft is ongoing or part of repeated incidents in your area. Keep the event/reference number for insurance and to support disputes with senders. Include dates, tracking info, and any CCTV details if available.

## Step 3 — Notify the sender immediately if sensitive documents were stolen

If the missing mail included bank cards, PINs, cheques, passports, Medicare letters, or government correspondence, contact the sender straight away and request cancellation/reissue. Ask them to place a security note on your account and confirm how they’ll deliver replacements (signature required, secure pickup, or alternate address). Don’t wait for investigations to finish before taking these steps.

## Step 4 — Contact IDCARE if identity or financial documents were involved

If the stolen mail could be used for identity checks (licence details, Medicare, TFN letters) or financial access, contact **IDCARE** for targeted advice and next actions.  

1800 595 160

## Step 5 — Request a mail hold or redirect if theft is ongoing

If theft appears ongoing, ask Australia Post about a **mail hold** or **redirect** option and consider a secure parcel collection point. Also check your letterbox security (locks, placement, visibility) and consider using registered delivery for sensitive documents. Keep records of dates and reference numbers for each report.
