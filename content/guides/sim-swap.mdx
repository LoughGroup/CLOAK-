If your mobile number was **ported or moved without your permission**, treat it as an emergency: SMS codes and some account resets may now go to someone else. Minutes matter.

## Step 1 — Call your telco immediately

Contact your carrier from another phone or line and say you suspect an **unauthorised port** or **SIM change**.

- **Telstra:** 13 22 00  
- **Optus:** 1300 300 937  
- **Vodafone:** 1300 650 410  

Ask what they can do **right now** to restore your number or block misuse on your account.

## Step 2 — Reverse the port and add a port freeze

Ask the telco to **reverse an unauthorised port** if that applies, and to place a **SIM lock** or **port freeze** (and any **PIN** or **extra verification** they offer) so it cannot happen again without proof it is you.

Confirm what confirmation you will receive and how to follow up if the number is not restored quickly.

## Step 3 — Change passwords on accounts that use SMS 2FA

For **banking, email, myGov, crypto, and any account** that sends login codes by SMS, assume the attacker may receive those codes. **Change passwords** (from a safe device), **revoke sessions**, and **switch to an authenticator app** or stronger second factor where the service allows it.

## Step 4 — Report to ACCC Scamwatch

Report the incident to **Scamwatch** so it is on record and contributes to broader scam intelligence.

- **Website:** [scamwatch.gov.au](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au)

Keep notes of dates, who you spoke to at the telco, and any reference numbers for police or your bank if needed later.
