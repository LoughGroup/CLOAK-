When a lost item turns up, take a moment to confirm it’s safe and unwind any emergency actions you started.

## Step 1 — Confirm it’s intact and not tampered with

Check for obvious signs of tampering, damage, or missing components before you resume normal use. If it’s a device, look for changed PINs, new SIMs, unknown profiles, or unexpected apps. If anything feels off, treat it as compromised and continue with security steps.

## Step 2 — If it’s a device, run a security check before reconnecting accounts

Before signing back into email, banking, or myGov, update the device OS and run security scans where available. Rotate key passwords if you haven’t already, and review account sign-in history for the period it was missing. If you used “Lost Mode”, disable it only after you’re confident the device is secure.

## Step 3 — Cancel replacements that haven’t been issued

If you ordered replacement cards, keys, or documents, contact the issuing organisation and ask what can be cancelled or paused. For identity documents, confirm whether a replacement process can be stopped once started, and whether the original will still be valid. Don’t undo protective steps (like credit bans) until you’re confident no misuse occurred.

## Step 4 — Notify police if you filed a report

If you lodged a police report, update them that the item was recovered and ask whether any further action is required. Provide the report/reference number and keep a note of who you spoke to. Closing the loop helps keep records accurate if issues arise later.

## Step 5 — Update your insurer if a claim was started

If you opened an insurance claim, inform your insurer that the item was found and discuss next steps (withdrawal, adjustment for damage, or partial reimbursement). Keep emails and reference numbers so there’s clarity on what was claimed and what was returned. If you were reimbursed, ask what the repayment or claim reversal process is.

## Step 6 — Review whether emergency account changes should be reverted

Consider whether temporary changes (new cards, new phone numbers, changed recovery settings) should remain for safety. If you replaced locks or keys, don’t revert them unless you’re sure the old key was never duplicated or misused. The goal is to return to normal without reintroducing the original risk.
