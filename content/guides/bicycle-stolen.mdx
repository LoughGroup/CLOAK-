With bike theft, speed helps: the sooner you report and search resale channels, the higher the chance of recovery.

## Step 1 — Report to police (include serial number if you have it)

Make a report with **police** (online where available, or call **131 444** for non-urgent assistance) and include the bike’s serial number, make/model, colour, and any unique marks. Provide photos if you have them and list any accessories stolen with it. Keep the event/reference number for insurance and follow-ups.

## Step 2 — Check whether the bike was registered

If you registered the bike (e.g. **BikeIndex.org** or a state/club registry), mark it as stolen and upload the police report details. If you didn’t register it, consider adding it now with the serial number and photos to support identification. Registration records can help if it’s recovered later.

## Step 3 — Search local resale listings

Check common resale channels like Facebook Marketplace and Gumtree for matching bikes and accessories. Save screenshots and listing links and avoid direct confrontation — report suspicious listings to the platform and police. If you arrange a viewing, do it with police guidance where possible.

## Step 4 — Notify your insurer (home and contents or specific bike cover)

Contact your insurer and provide the police event number, purchase receipts, and photos. Ask what evidence they need for valuation (especially for e-bikes and upgraded components). Keep a list of stolen accessories such as locks, lights, batteries, and mounts.

## Step 5 — Post in local community groups

Share a clear photo, description, and serial number (if you’re comfortable) in local cycling groups and community pages. Include the suburb and time window of theft and ask people to message you privately with sightings. The goal is to increase visibility without escalating personal risk.
