A stolen driver licence is often used to pass identity checks, so the goal is to get it **flagged and replaced** quickly and watch for follow-on fraud.

## Step 1 — Report it to police

Make a report with **police** (online where available, or call **131 444** for non-urgent assistance). Ask for an **event/reference number** and keep it with your notes. This helps if the licence is later linked to fraud or other offences.

## Step 2 — Contact your state roads authority for a replacement

Report the theft to your **state or territory roads/transport authority** and request a replacement licence under their stolen-ID process. Ask whether they can **flag the old licence number** and what documentation you’ll need. If your state issues a new card number, update it anywhere you use it for verification.

## Step 3 — Notify your bank (and any services that verified you with it)

Call your bank and tell them your driver licence was stolen and may be used for identity verification attempts. Ask them to note your file and confirm any extra protection they can add to phone banking or in-branch ID checks. If you recently used the licence to set up accounts (telco, BNPL, finance), consider notifying those providers too.

## Step 4 — Place a credit alert with the bureaus

Place alerts/bans with all three credit bureaus to make it harder for new credit to be opened in your name:

- **Equifax:** 138 332  
- **Experian:** 1300 783 684  
- **illion:** 132 333  

Record confirmation details and the expiry date, and extend if needed.

## Step 5 — Monitor myGov and identity touchpoints

Sign in to **myGov** from a trusted device and review linked services (ATO, Medicare, Centrelink) for contact-detail changes or unfamiliar activity. Turn on stronger sign-in options if available and check recovery details on your primary email account. If you see signs of misuse, escalate to **IDCARE** (1800 595 160) for guidance.
