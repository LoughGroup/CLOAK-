If your phone is lost (not clearly stolen), act quickly to protect your accounts while you try to recover it safely.

## Step 1 — Put the phone in Lost Mode

On iPhone, use **Find My** (or [icloud.com/find](https://www.icloud.com/find)) to mark it as lost, lock it, and display a message with a contact number. On Android, use **Find My Device** at [google.com/android/find](https://www.google.com/android/find) to secure it and show contact info. This reduces casual access while you work through the next steps.

## Step 2 — Call your number from another phone

Call your mobile number from a trusted phone to see if someone answers, and listen for voicemail or ringing that helps you narrow down where it might be. If it’s in a venue (taxi, café, workplace), ask staff to check lost property without sharing sensitive details. Avoid meeting strangers alone — prioritise safety over recovery.

## Step 3 — Ask your carrier to flag the IMEI

Contact your telco and ask them to **flag the handset IMEI** as lost, and confirm what that means for blocking service if it turns into theft. Carriers can also advise whether a SIM replacement is needed and what extra security (PIN/port protections) you can add. Keep any reference number they give you.

## Step 4 — Check the last known location (and recent sign-ins)

Use Find My / Find My Device to review the **last known location** and any movement patterns before you take further action. In parallel, check your email and key accounts for recent sign-in alerts from the time you lost the phone. If anything looks suspicious, start changing passwords immediately from a safe device.

## Step 5 — Report to police if not found within 24 hours

If you can’t recover it within a day, lodge a report with **police** (online where available, or call **131 444** for non-urgent assistance). A report number can help with insurers and carrier processes. If you think it was taken in front of you or there’s immediate danger, call **000**.

## Step 6 — Change critical passwords if not recovered within 48 hours

If the phone isn’t back within two days, assume it may be accessed and rotate passwords starting with **email**, then **banking**, then any accounts that use SMS codes. Revoke active sessions and switch to stronger multi-factor options (authenticator app or passkeys) where available. If you use a password manager on the device, update that master password from a trusted device first.
