When something goes wrong overseas, focus on safety and official support first, then paperwork and insurance.

## Step 1 — Contact the nearest Australian Embassy or Consulate

If you need urgent consular assistance, contact the nearest **Australian Embassy or Consulate** and explain what happened and what you need (documents, safety advice, local contacts). For 24-hour Consular Emergency Centre support, use the official number below.  

+61 2 6261 3305

## Step 2 — Register with Smartraveller (or update your details)

If you haven’t already, register your trip with **Smartraveller** and ensure your contact and itinerary details are current. This can help with safety advisories and makes it easier for consular services to assist.  

[smartraveller.gov.au](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au)

## Step 3 — Report to local police and obtain a written report

Report the incident to local police and ask for a **written report** (or official reference number) in the local format. This is commonly required for insurance claims and replacement documents, and it creates a clear timeline. Keep copies/photos of the report and any receipts for associated costs.

## Step 4 — Contact your travel insurer immediately

Call your travel insurer’s emergency line and open a case as early as possible. Provide police report details, hospital/clinic info if relevant, and any receipts or booking references. Ask what they can arrange directly (medical providers, accommodation changes, replacement flights) and what expenses they will reimburse.

## Step 5 — If your passport was stolen, seek an emergency travel document

If your passport is stolen or missing and you need to travel, ask the consulate about an **emergency travel document** and the documents required to issue it. Start the passport-theft steps in parallel (cancellation and reporting) so the original passport can’t be misused. Keep passport photos and identity evidence securely stored if you have them.

## Step 6 — Notify family or your emergency contact in Australia

Contact a trusted person in Australia and give them a brief summary, your current location, and any reference numbers. Ask them to hold copies of key documents and to help coordinate calls if you’re overwhelmed. Keeping someone informed reduces risk and helps if you need support quickly.
