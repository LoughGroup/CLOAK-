A lost access card or fob is a security issue because it can grant entry without forcing doors or triggering alarms.

## Step 1 — Report it to building management or your security desk

Tell building management, concierge, or your employer’s security desk as soon as you notice it missing. Provide the card/fob ID if you know it and where you last used it. Ask for a reference number or ticket so there’s a clear record of when you reported it.

## Step 2 — Request deactivation immediately

Ask for the card to be **deactivated** right away, ideally before you leave the building or site. Confirm when deactivation takes effect and whether any doors have offline readers that might not update instantly. If the card also grants lift access or parking entry, ensure those permissions are removed too.

## Step 3 — Report to police if stolen

If the card was stolen (not just misplaced), report it to **police** and keep the event/reference number for building management and insurers if needed. This can also help if the card is later used in an incident. For non-urgent reporting, many states use online reporting or **131 444**.

## Step 4 — Request a replacement and review access logs

Arrange a replacement card/fob and ask whether security can review access logs around the time you lost it. If there were entries you didn’t authorise, escalate immediately through your workplace or building incident process. Update any internal systems that track your badge number if required.

## Step 5 — Trigger a security review if sensitive areas were accessible

If the card granted access to server rooms, medication stores, cash handling, or restricted workspaces, ask for a short security review. This may include changing PIN codes, reviewing CCTV, or temporarily increasing checks at entry points. The goal is to reduce risk even if the card is later found.
