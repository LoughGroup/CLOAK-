When a pet goes missing, early visibility and fast contact with the right local services can make a big difference.

## Step 1 — Search the immediate area and alert neighbours

Search on foot and by car around your home, focusing on hiding spots, parks, and nearby streets. Ask neighbours to check garages, sheds, and under decks, and leave a contact number. If it’s safe, bring a familiar item (treats, favourite toy) to help draw your pet out.

## Step 2 — Contact your local council ranger

Call your local council and ask to speak with the ranger or animal management team. Provide a description, microchip number (if known), and where/when the pet was last seen. Ask which pounds or holding facilities they use and how quickly animals are listed online or over the phone.

## Step 3 — Report to your state pet registry

Report the pet missing with the registry used in your state (e.g. **NSW Pet Registry**, **PetAddress**, **Central Animal Records** depending on state/territory). Ensure your contact details on the microchip record are correct so you can be reached quickly. If you’ve recently moved or changed numbers, update that immediately.

## Step 4 — Post in local groups and Lost Pet Finders Australia

Post clear photos and a short description in local Facebook community groups and any suburb-specific “lost and found pets” pages. Also post to **Lost Pet Finders Australia** and include a contact method and the last known location/time. Avoid sharing your full street address publicly — use private messages for exact location details.

## Step 5 — Contact nearby vets and shelters

Call (and if possible visit) nearby vets and animal shelters with a photo and description. Ask them to scan for microchips if someone brings in an animal matching your pet. Keep a list of who you contacted and when, so you can follow up efficiently.

## Step 6 — Check your microchip details are up to date

Once your pet is found (or while searching), confirm your microchip record has current phone numbers and addresses. If your pet is a repeat escape artist, consider updating tags, harness/collar fit, and safe containment at home. Quick identification reduces time spent in shelters and increases the chance of safe return.
