After a wallet theft in Australia, assume **cards and ID** could be used quickly. The goals are to **stop spending**, **invalidate stolen ID documents**, and create a **paper trail** for insurers and disputes.

## Step 1 — Call your bank immediately to cancel all cards

Ring your bank’s **fraud or lost-cards line** (or use the app’s **block card** feature if you cannot reach the phone queue fast enough, then follow up by phone). Ask them to **cancel or block every card** that was in the wallet.

Have them **check recent transactions** and dispute anything you did not authorise. Note the **reference number** for the call.

## Step 2 — Report your driver licence stolen to your state roads authority

Report the theft to your **state or territory roads / transport department** (e.g. Service NSW, VicRoads, Transport and Main Roads Queensland, etc.) so the **licence can be flagged or replaced** according to their process. This reduces the chance someone uses your licence number for fraud.

## Step 3 — Report your Medicare card stolen to Services Australia

If your **Medicare card** was in the wallet, tell **Services Australia** so the card can be **cancelled and reissued**.

- **Phone:** **132 011** (check [servicesaustralia.gov.au](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au) for current hours and alternatives)

Also review your **myGov** sign-in and linked services once your documents are under control.

## Step 4 — File a police report for insurance purposes

Report the theft to **police** and keep the **event or reference number**. Insurers and some banks ask for it, and it helps if fraudulent accounts or purchases appear later.
