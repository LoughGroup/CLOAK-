When house keys are lost or stolen, the key question is whether someone could identify your address and access your home.

## Step 1 — Assess the risk (was your address attached?)

Work out whether the keys were lost with anything that reveals your address (mail, licence, a labelled keyring, gym tag, or parked car nearby). If the keys were stolen, assume intent and treat it as high risk. This risk assessment determines how urgently you should rekey or replace locks.

## Step 2 — Contact a licensed locksmith to rekey or replace locks

If there’s any chance the keys could be used to find your home, contact a **licensed locksmith** to **rekey** or replace external door locks as soon as possible. Rekeying is often quicker and cheaper than full lock replacement while still invalidating the lost key. Ask for an invoice/receipt in case your insurer covers the callout.

## Step 3 — Notify your property manager or body corporate (if applicable)

If you rent, notify your **property manager/landlord** promptly and follow your lease process for lock changes. If you live in an apartment complex, inform the **body corporate/building manager** if keys or fobs could grant common-area access. Confirm whether they need to disable any building access tied to your keys.

## Step 4 — Check home and contents insurance for locksmith cover

Many policies include limited cover for emergency locksmith services after theft or loss. Call your insurer (or check your policy documents) to confirm what’s covered, what evidence they require, and whether a police report is needed. Keep photos of the locks replaced and any receipts.

## Step 5 — Report to police if stolen

If the keys were stolen (or taken during a burglary), report it to **police** and record the **event/reference number**. This can help with insurance claims and establishes a timeline if there’s later unauthorised entry. For non-urgent assistance, you can call **131 444**.
