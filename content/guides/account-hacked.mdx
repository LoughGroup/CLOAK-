A compromised account — email, social, or banking — needs a tight sequence so you cut off the attacker and keep control. Work through these steps as soon as you can from a **trusted device** and network.

## Step 1 — Use the platform’s account recovery

Go to the service’s official app or website (type the address yourself; avoid links in suspicious messages) and start **account recovery** or **forgot password**. Prefer options that send a reset to a **backup email** or **authenticator** you still control.

If you are locked out, use the provider’s **account recovery** or **support** flow and keep any case or reference numbers.

## Step 2 — Change your email password immediately

If the hacked account used your **primary email** for logins or resets, change that **email password first** (before or right alongside other accounts). Review recovery addresses and phone numbers in the email settings and remove anything you do not recognise.

## Step 3 — Revoke all active sessions

In the compromised service’s **security** or **privacy** settings, **sign out of all devices** or **revoke active sessions**. Then sign back in only on your own devices and check for unfamiliar locations or apps with access.

## Step 4 — Enable authenticator app 2FA — not SMS

Turn on **multi-factor authentication** using an **authenticator app** (or hardware security keys where offered). **Avoid SMS-only 2FA** for high-value accounts where possible — SIM-related attacks can intercept codes.

Save **backup codes** in a secure place in case you lose your phone.
