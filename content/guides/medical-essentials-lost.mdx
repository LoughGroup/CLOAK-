If critical medication or medical supplies are lost or stolen, prioritise continuity of care first, then deal with reports and replacement.

## Step 1 — Contact your GP or prescribing doctor for an emergency script

Call your GP or the prescribing doctor and explain that your medication was lost or stolen and you need an urgent replacement. Ask whether they can issue an **electronic prescription** to your phone/email or send directly to a pharmacy. If you’re away from home, tell them your current location so they can advise the best option.

## Step 2 — If after-hours, contact the National Home Doctor Service

If it’s outside normal clinic hours, contact the **National Home Doctor Service** and ask about urgent prescription support and the quickest pathway for your medication type.

## Step 3 — Go to a pharmacy and ask about emergency supply

Present to a pharmacy and explain the situation; many pharmacies can provide an **emergency supply** in some circumstances while confirming details with your GP. Bring any evidence you have (old script label, medication box, pharmacy records) to speed things up. Ask what they need to document the supply and what follow-up script they require.

## Step 4 — Escalate immediately for insulin or other critical medication

If the medication is time-critical (e.g. insulin) and you’re at risk of harm, call **000** or go to the nearest emergency department. Don’t wait for phone queues if symptoms or health risk are escalating. Your safety and stabilisation comes first.

## Step 5 — Report to police if stolen (and document what was taken)

If the items were stolen, report to **police** and keep the event/reference number for insurers and providers. Write down exactly what was taken (medication names, dosage, devices) and when/where it occurred. This helps with replacement and any investigation, and prevents confusion in later medical care.
